# Biomass-Anaerobic-Digestion-CHP
Integrated biomass, anaerobic digestion, and CHP system for circular energy management in food processing industries
A conceptual engineering design and feasibility study for converting **60 tonnes/day of mixed organic food-processing waste** into renewable electricity, thermal energy, and useful by-products.

---

## 📌 Project Overview

The food-processing industry generates significant amounts of organic waste while requiring substantial amounts of electricity and thermal energy.

This project proposes an integrated **circular waste-to-energy system** that combines:

- ♻️ **Anaerobic Digestion (AD)**
- 🔥 **Biomass Combustion**
- ⚡ **Combined Heat and Power (CHP)**

The system is designed to convert organic waste into renewable energy while reducing landfill disposal, fossil-fuel dependence, and greenhouse gas emissions.

---

## 🎯 Project Objectives

- Convert organic food-processing waste into renewable energy
- Reduce dependence on fossil fuels
- Generate electricity and useful thermal energy
- Reduce greenhouse gas emissions
- Minimize organic waste sent to landfill
- Recover useful by-products such as digestate and biomass ash
- Evaluate technical, operational, environmental, and financial feasibility
- Demonstrate a circular economy approach for food-processing facilities

---

## ⚙️ Proposed System

### 1. Anaerobic Digestion

High-moisture organic waste is processed through anaerobic digestion.

The process produces:

- **Biogas**
- **Digestate**

Biogas is used as a fuel source for the CHP system, while digestate can potentially be utilized as a nutrient-rich agricultural product.

### 2. Biomass Combustion

Dry fibrous residues such as:

- Nut shells
- Husks
- Dry pulp
- Wood-like residues

are directed toward biomass combustion to generate thermal energy and reduce natural-gas requirements.

### 3. Combined Heat and Power (CHP)

Biogas is used in the CHP system to simultaneously generate:

- ⚡ Electricity
- ♨️ Recoverable thermal energy

The project estimates a combined CHP efficiency of approximately **75–80%**.
